# Sources: "What if friction disappeared for 5 seconds?"

| On screen | Source | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Standing perfectly still needs no friction | Newton's first law: with no horizontal force there's nothing to resist | Walking requires a horizontal push against the ground (the required coefficient of friction is measured at ~0.4–0.5 when pushing off while turning, ASU) |
| Knots hold only because of friction | Patil et al., "Topological mechanics of knots and tangles", *Science* 367 (2020) | |

## What the scene does
- Walkers: their feet slide apart (splits), and they fall while still gliding at walking speed. Nothing stops them until the grip comes back.
- People standing on the red steps don't fall.
- Cars: brakes don't work, so they keep going at the same speed. When the grip returns, the brake pedal is already pressed and they skid to a stop.
