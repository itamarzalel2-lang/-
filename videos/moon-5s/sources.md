# Sources: "What if the Moon came 10× closer for 5 seconds?"

| On screen | Value | Source / calculation |
|---|---|---|
| Moon's distance | 384,400 km average | NASA Moon fact sheet |
| Tidal force ∝ 1/distance³ → 10× closer = 1,000× | 1000× | NOAA National Ocean Service, "Tides and Water Levels: Gravity, Inertia, and the Two Bulges" (the tide-generating force is inversely proportional to the cube of the distance) |
| A tide needs ~6 hours | ~6 h 12 min between high and low | NOAA: most coasts get two high and two low tides every lunar day (24 h 50 min) |
| Closer than TV satellites | 38,440 km vs 42,164 km | 384,400 ÷ 10 = 38,440 km (center to center). Geostationary orbit radius is 42,164 km from Earth's center (35,786 km above the surface) |

## Visual
At 10× closer the Moon is 10× wider in angle (0.52° → 5.2°), 100× the area, and the moonlight is 100× stronger. That's why the street turns silver-blue.

## Correction during writing
The draft said "it would still be outside the orbit of the TV satellites". That's wrong: the 35,786 km figure is altitude above the surface, while 38,440 km is from the center. The text was corrected to "closer than TV satellites".
