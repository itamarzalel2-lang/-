# Sources: "What if gravity switched off for 5 seconds?"

| On screen | Value | Source / calculation | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Springs squeezed by the car's weight | ~17 cm | Ride frequency of passenger cars ≈ 1–1.5 Hz (comfort range) · static deflection δ = g/(2πf)² = 9.81/(2π·1.2)² ≈ 0.17 m. [RTA journal (PDF)](https://journals.rta.lv/index.php/ETR/article/download/8010/6320/10025) · [AutoSpeed](https://blog.autospeed.com/2015/05/10/sprung-and-unsprung-weight-natural-frequencies/) | Ranges from ~11 cm (1.5 Hz) to ~25 cm (1 Hz) |
| Launch speed ~0.8 m/s, ~4 m up after 5 s | ~4 m | A released spring gives the body √(g·δ) ≈ 1.3 m/s. The dampers absorb some energy and the wheels get dragged along, so ≈ 0.8 m/s. 0.8 m/s × 5 s = 4 m | Estimate; the card says "~" |
| A 4 m drop = ~32 km/h | 8.9 m/s | Free fall: v = √(2gh) = √(2·9.81·4) ≈ 8.86 m/s ≈ 32 km/h | |

## Physics shown without numbers
- With no gravity there's no normal force, so no friction either. Moving cars keep going in a straight line at the same speed, and the brakes don't help until they land.
- People mid-step (≈0.1–0.3 m/s upward) float up a meter or so, then drop back.
- The air: in 5 seconds the atmosphere doesn't escape. The expansion starts at the top and doesn't reach the ground in that time.
