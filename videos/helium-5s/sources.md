# Sources: "What if all the air turned into helium for 5 seconds?"

| In the narration / on screen | Value | Source | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sound travels almost 3× faster | 1,007 m/s vs 343 m/s (20 °C) | Ideal gas: c = √(γRT/M) with γ = 5/3, M = 4 g/mol → 1,007 m/s · [HyperPhysics, sound speed in gases](https://hyperphysics.gsu.edu/hbase/Sound/souspe3.html) | Some tables list 927–972 m/s (different temperatures) |
| The pitch doesn't change, the resonance does | n/a | Joe Wolfe, UNSW Music Acoustics, "Speech and helium speech" [phys.unsw.edu.au](https://www.phys.unsw.edu.au/jw/speechmodel.html) · HKUST Science Focus | The helium voice in the scene is a stand-in effect (a same-length shift up) |
| Helium is 7× lighter than air | 0.166 vs 1.2 kg/m³ | ρ = PM/RT · [Engineering ToolBox](https://engineeringtoolbox.com/amp/helium-density-specific-weight-temperature-pressure-d_2090.html) | Wing lift is proportional to gas density |

## Physics shown without numbers
- Engines stall (there's no oxygen in helium), and drivers brake.
- Car horns ring higher too (the horn's resonance chamber is "in helium"), as does the crowd noise.
- In 5 seconds no one passes out (as in the air video: ~10 s of consciousness).
